For Mightybytes, a good customer experience starts with active listening and verifying values alignment. It’s important we do both. 

Active listening helps us craft better proposals and, eventually, design better solutions.

Values alignment helps us nurture trust and build long-term, mutual success.

These are core stepping stones to how we design meaningful customer experiences. Our clients have chosen Mightybytes as their partner. That's not a decision we take lightly. 

Designing Winning Customer Experiences

CX at Mightybytes is intentionally designed and thoughtfully executed. This entails blending common experience and service design practices with a real-world commitment to building trust and nurturing mutual respect while helping them achieve their goals. 

These rigorous practices help us design winning customer experiences that improve collaboration and drive long-term, shared success over time driven by target outcomes rather than simply a checklist of outputs.

With this in mind, most of our client relationships have lasted five years or more. We maintain this track record by creating long-term, shared value between our organizations and rigorously focusing on trust and customer experience. Service design activities help us achieve this.

Good collaboration and intentional Service Design practices drive how we create better customer experiences.

Embracing Service Design Practices

To this end, user experience design practices have been part of Mightybytes' toolkit for decades now. Related, service design practices help us improve customer and other stakeholder experiences. Service Design includes many exercises and activities grounded in Design Thinking and related disciplines. 

Some of these activities include:

Systems mapping

Contextual interviews and ethnographic research

Brainstorming, card sorting, clustering, ranking, dot voting, and other ideation methods

Current- and future-state journey and experience mapping

Service blueprints

Prototyping and wireframing

Scenario planning and investigative rehearsals

And many more…

These activities help us better understand how customers and other stakeholders perceive a service or program we hope to design or improve. Knowing when to employ each depends on the context of what we aim to achieve.

However, in every case, Service Design activities drive tactical and measurable CX improvements that build trust. 

Nurturing Shared Prosperity

We take being a customer advocate seriously. It also requires us to think differently about how we design systems to create shared value. 

Here are several ways we help our clients create change and align our work together with their mission and a larger vision for shared prosperity.

1. Sustainability & Whole Systems Thinking

You can’t sweat the right details unless you clearly understand the big picture. That’s why we incorporate systems thinking into our discovery process, so we understand your entire organization and where it sits within a larger ecosystem. 

This helps us make better recommendations across the board and allows us to view client challenges from multiple angles, including the perspective of less obvious stakeholders like local communities, the environment, and so on. 

Sure, we conduct user research and create personas just like any digital agency, but we also ask deeper questions:

How does the organization fit into a larger social, ecological, and economic system?

Do opportunities exist to foster innovation or transform an organization with this project?

Can we help them incorporate more responsible digital practices within their organization?

Where are the collective impact possibilities between our organizational mission and theirs?

Can we identify potential paths to achieving long-term growth, value, and impact? What about impediments?

Planet as stakeholder: Can we power their digital products and services with renewable energy?

How can we create mutual, long-term success for all project stakeholders beyond the initial scope of work?

By asking these (and other) questions up front during the discovery process and revisiting them throughout a project, we can identify opportunities that support better outcomes for everyone.

2. Building Mutual Trust

Change and learning are continuous throughout any relationship. Individual projects are no different. We embrace continuous learning because it yields superior solutions, fosters collaboration, and builds trust.

The latter is key. While great products and good collaboration are important, without mutual trust any relationship will eventually fail, so we work hard to build and maintain it:

We listen intently and ask many qualifying questions (see above).

Conversely, we are always available to answer questions and alert our clients whenever any new piece of information could impact a deliverable timeline or budget.

Clear communication and collaboration: workshops, regular check-ins, and in-person meetings that focus specifically on relationship-building as noted above.

We are transparent about budget, scope, deliverables, timing, and so on.

We are willing to pivot when necessary and share risk with our clients on projects.

In addition to the list above, we also regularly improve client onboarding processes to set clear expectations, answer important questions up front, and minimize surprises.

4. Embracing Tension

All this said, any long-term relationship runs the risk of turning sour, despite everyone’s best intentions. We think we’re pretty good at listening and asking qualifying questions, but sometimes it’s easy to misinterpret things. Digital projects are often complicated, leaving numerous opportunities for miscommunication.

So, how do you maintain value while also navigating uncertain waters and keeping everyone happy? Embrace the tension. Lean into it. 

In our experience, project hiccups are either about simple misunderstandings, which are generally easy to navigate, or mismanaged expectations, which often aren’t. By designing processes with built-in opportunities to release steam and course-correct, navigating these situations becomes easier.

Marketing tech, and the practices that accompany it, change all the time. It can be tough to keep up. This requires clear, ongoing communication and mutual trust. It's okay to disagree as long as we're all working toward the same goal.

— Jeff Marcous, Founder, Dharma Merchant Services

Despite having processes in place and nearly 30 years experience building hundreds of complex websites and digital products, we do occasionally miss something. We’re only human. The most important thing is that we learn from these situations so as not to repeat them.

We’re not afraid to have difficult conversations so long as the target outcome is a mutual understanding by both parties on the best path forward.

Frequently Asked Customer Experience Questions

Finally, here are answers to some of the most commonly asked questions about using service design to improve customer experiences. 

What is service design and how do I know if I need it? Service design is the practice of creating the communications, infrastructure, and governance policies and practices necessary to deliver better services that improve customer experiences. Key indicators that you might need service design include regular customer complaints, team confusion around processes, or mistrust among organizational stakeholders.

 How does service design drive better customer experiences? Understanding how people feel during their interactions with your organization, especially in product, service, or program delivery, opens up ample opportunities to improve customer experiences, both in-person and through digital channels. 

Service design practices like preparatory research, journey or experience mapping, investigative rehearsals, prototyping, stakeholder interviews, and others reveal important customer experience insights. These enable your team to improve relationships so people feel excited to do business with you. 

 Why should we put resources into customer experience? According to Edelman's Trust Barometer, trust in organizations, especially businesses, is continuing a record-breaking downward slide. AI and social media algorithms only make things worse. Investing in customer experience shows you care about those with whom you conduct business, which can build trust. 

 How can I convince leadership to invest in service design? Track customer churn over time and research why they leave your organization for another solution. Identify trends or patterns, then estimate the hard costs associated with this churn. Chances are, this will make a clear case to improve customer experience. Service design offers compelling ways to achieve this. 

 What are the benefits of improving customer experience through service design? The benefits of using service design to improve customer experience are many. First, it aligns your team around a shared blueprint for success. Testing and prototyping early also reduces costly reworks. Plus, effective service design links customer satisfaction to retention, revenue, and other important metrics.

Finally, good service design improves consistency. Customers don't care whether they're on your app, website, or talking to a human. Service design ensures the experience feels coherent wherever they interact with you.
